# Transit 5E-5 telemetry-format archive requests

## 0. Already-released Navy FOIA case for the observed object

The Navy's [FY 2023 FOIA log, p. 43](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/foia/readingroom/CaseFiles/FOIA%20Logs/FY%2023%20FOIA%20Log_Redacted.pdf) records case **DON-NAVY-2023-015194** (received 21 August 2023). Its request names **Transit 5B-5, 1964-083D, NORAD 965** and explicitly seeks telemetry decoding, data structure, modulation, and telemetry-transmitter electronics. The disposition is **"Full Release"** and status **"Closed (Documents Sent)"**. The log does not list or reproduce the responsive documents, so it does **not** prove that a channel/bit map was released. Web searches for the case number and title found the log but no public response package.

Follow-up search on 26 October 2026: exact case-number variants, quoted request text, Navy reading-room URLs, MuckRock and Internet Archive indexes yielded no response letter, attachment inventory or released files. The Navy reading-room page returned a connection reset in the in-app browser. Treat the package as **released to the original requester, not yet located online**; do not cite the log as proof of its contents.

**First acquisition request:** ask the [Navy FOIA service center](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/foia/contact/SitePages/Home.aspx) for the final response letter, inventory and the exact previously released records in case `DON-NAVY-2023-015194`. If they were not placed in the public reading room, ask how to obtain a copy of the same released package. Identify the observed radio source as NORAD 965 / 1964-083D, independently of any disputed physical 5E-5 versus Oscar-2 attribution. The source's Doppler match to 965 does not by itself settle that historical naming issue. The case may contain only already-known general reports; inspect its inventory before treating it as a new format document.

These are targeted research requests for the **science telemetry of Transit 5E-5 / 1964-083C / NORAD 959**, received around **136.65 MHz**. Its co-launched navigation satellite **Transit 5BN-5 / Oscar 2 / 1964-083D** has a different data system. Do not use the AN/UYK-1 navigation computer format as a substitute.

## 1. NSSDCA data and documentation request

Send to the [NSSDCA Coordinated Request and User Support Office (CRUSO)](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/about/about_cruso.html): `gsfc-dl-nssdca-request@mail.nasa.gov`. The newer [NSSDCA data-access page](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nssdc/obtaining_data.html) also lists the general contact `gsfc-dl-nssdca-contact-us@mail.nasa.gov` if the CRUSO address does not respond. The archived [dataset catalog](https://spdf.gsfc.nasa.gov/pub/documents/old/documentation_from_nssdc/renamed_dataset_catalogs/transit5e5_mag_64-083c-01a_DSC_0032.pdf) gives **64-083C-01A**, **D000242**, **DR004385**, and **DS004385**. Its scanned pages identify a processed magnetometer tape (1333 80-character records), supplied by Favin and Zmuda and created on an IBM 7094. Ask for both the restored data and associated documentation, but distinguish these reduced data from raw telemetry.

**Subject:** Transit 5E-5 / 1964-083C telemetry documentation and dataset 64-083C-01A

> Dear NSSDCA team,
>
> I am researching the scientific telemetry of Transit 5E-5 (1964-083C, NORAD 959). Your catalog identifies the rubidium magnetometer dataset 64-083C-01A, original dataset D000242, restored media DR004385 / DS004385, covering 17 Dec 1964–30 Jun 1965.
>
> Could you provide access to the restored data/AIP and its complete documentation, including the 80-character record layout, variable definitions, units, calibration and processing notes, and any original document IDs? Please also check whether the archive holds earlier processing levels: raw received telemetry, time-tagged/"Dictionary I" data, pass tapes, or records for other 5E-5 instruments.
>
> In particular, I seek the original 5E-5 PCM frame/word schedule, PAM commutator channel assignments, command/telltale bit definitions, and the IBM 7094 decommutation/processing program or listing. The magnetometer catalog says its reduced tape was created on a 7094; I do not assume that tape itself contains the raw channel map. If these records were not transferred to NSSDCA, could you identify the accession, originating APL group or current custodian?
>
> Thank you for any catalog identifiers or referrals you can provide.

## 2. APL historical engineering records

Use the [APL contact form](https://www.jhuapl.edu/contact) and ask that the message be routed to the APL archivist / historical records staff. A [Johns Hopkins staff page](https://advanced.jhu.edu/directory/jordon-steele/) identifies Jordon Steele as an APL archivist. The specific organizational lead is the **Telemetry and Command Group** in the 1964 APL Space Department.

**Subject:** Archival request: Transit 5E-5 (1964-083C) telemetry format and decommutator

> Dear APL Archives,
>
> I am trying to reconstruct the scientific telemetry of APL satellite Transit 5E-5 (1964-083C, launched December 1964), specifically its 136.65-MHz analog PAM and digital PCM transmission. Could you search the 1963–1966 records of the Space Department's Telemetry and Command Group, Data and Control Branch, project engineering and ground data-processing teams?
>
> The most useful items would be a flight telemetry format or encoder/interface drawing; 16-bit PCM word and subframe schedules; assignments for the three 35-channel analog commutators and any subcommutation; command and telltale/status-bit truth tables; receiver/decommutator manuals; calibration sheets; and Pulse Data Processor / IBM 7094 Dictionary I or II program listings and input/output dictionaries for 5E-5. A surviving pass printout with channel headings would also help. Please include any revisions or mode notes explaining the historical ~195.3-bit/s rate versus ~390.6 bit/s observed in later transmissions.
>
> An APL report on predecessor 5E-1 (1963-038C), report S1P-86-65, describes a Pulse Data Processor and an IBM 7094 Dictionary I program that decommutated science telemetry. I seek the corresponding 5E-5 material, since the payload and commutator configuration differed. NSSDCA identifies a 5E-5 magnetometer tape (D000242 / 64-083C-01A) as having been produced on an IBM 7094 by the APL team; its catalog names Favin and Zmuda.
>
> Two 5E-5 report cross-references may help locate the relevant project files: magnetometer memorandum TG-890 / AD 652 807 and solar-cell/transistor memorandum TG-886 / AD 657 155 (March 1967; Fischell, Martin, Radford and Allen). TG-890 names Navy contract NOw 62-0604-c and the Special Projects Office in its distribution list. I already have TG-890 and SDO-1600; I seek the underlying telemetry/interface records rather than another copy of those reports.
>
> If these items are indexed under another project/report number or were transferred elsewhere, please advise on the collection, accession and access route.

## Evidence and interpretation

- Two further citations screened on 27 October 2026 are about other parts of the Transit program rather than the 5E-5 science telemetry. W. H. Guier, “Satellite Navigation Using Integral Doppler Data—The AN/SRN-9 Equipment,” *Journal of Geophysical Research* 71(24), 5903–5910 (1966), DOI `10.1029/JZ071i024p05903`, describes a **shipboard Doppler receiver and navigation computer**, not the spacecraft science telemetry encoder. Its references identify APL TG-525 (AN/SRN-9 groups) and TG-590 (simplified Doppler navigation); a NASA bibliography identifies an earlier version, APL TG-690 / AD 468 486. G. Bradshaw, F. Postula, and W. Hagis, “Description and Status of the Transit Thermoelectric Converter,” in the **1972** 7th Intersociety Energy Conversion Engineering Conference proceedings, pp. 200–215, concerns the later Transit RTG / TRIAD navigation power system, not the 1964 5E-5 solar-powered science telemetry. `A73-22751` is the accession for the 1543-page proceedings volume, not a distinct accession for that paper. These citations should not displace the 5E-5 format search or be added to the archive emails as if they identified the needed bit/channel map.
- J. W. Hamblen and J. B. Oakes, “Instrumentation and Telemetry of Transit Navigational Satellites,” *Electronics* 34 (11 August 1961), pp. 148–153, is in the [scanned issue](https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Electronics/60s/61/Electronics-1961-08-11.pdf); the complete six pages were inspected in the user's local copy `/home/jankoch/Downloads/Electronics-1961-08-11.pdf` (PDF pages 140–145). It distinguishes **Transit 1B** (pp. 150–151: seven thermistors plus a fixed calibration resistance, eight time-division pulse-width channels on the 162-MHz Doppler carrier; telemetry deliberately sparse) from **Transit 2-A** (pp. 150–152: separate ~108.06-MHz transmitter, three continuous measurement subcarriers around 2.3, 3.9, and 5.4 kHz, eight-channel temperature PDM on 10.5 kHz, clock-synchronized readout every 11.2 s). Figure 6 depicts the encoder/multiplexer; Figure 7 shows pulse-width coding and a calibration interval; Figure 8 shows a daylight-to-darkness pass with solar charging current, solar-panel voltage and auxiliary experiment traces. Page 153 explicitly credits **C. A. Blackburn and F. K. Preikschat** with designing the telemetry encoder, giving a useful APL personnel/engineering-file lead. The cited follow-on source R. W. Rochell, “Earth Satellite Telemetering Coding Systems,” *Electrical Engineering* 78(12), p. 1062 (December 1957), may explain the design family. This article predates 5E-5 by three years and does **not** provide the 5E-5 science PCM/PAM word/channel map; its timings/frequencies must not be imported into the decoder without independent evidence.
- The APL 5E-1 report `/home/jankoch/Downloads/19660004280.pdf` explicitly describes the PDP → IBM 1401 → IBM 7094 Dictionary I → Dictionary II chain. This supports the *kind* of record to request, not a claim that the 5E-5 layout was identical.
- The NSSDCA catalog `/home/jankoch/Downloads/transit5e5_mag_64-083c-01a_DSC_0032.pdf` documents a reduced scalar-field dataset and sample printouts, not the RF/PCM/PAM bit layout.
- The APL overview `/home/jankoch/Dokumente/recv/SDO1600.pdf` describes 5E-5 as having three 35-channel commutators, some analog subcommutation, a 256-bit digital encoder and eight on/off commands, without the individual assignments.
- Its Appendix C, Table C-2, entry 18 cites TG-886, *Radiation Damage to Orbiting Solar Cells and Transistors* (March 1967); a [NASA bibliography](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700011921/downloads/19700011921.pdf) gives the accession AD 657 155. This is an experiment-document lead, not a confirmed source of channel assignments.

## 3. Navy archives and declassified records

The [Naval History and Heritage Command Archives](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/archives.html) accepts public research inquiries at `NHHC-Archives@us.navy.mil`. Ask first for a **finding aid or accession reference** for APL Space Department / Bureau of Naval Weapons records on Transit 5E-5 (also search 5E-2, whose experiments were reproduced on 5E-5), especially the telemetry format, ground-station decommutation, engineering drawings, command tables and raw pass records. Include identifiers `1964-083C`, `S/N 43`, `AD652807 / TG-890`, `AD657155 / TG-886`, `SDO-1600 / ADA066299`, `D000242`, and `NOw 62-0604-c`; distinguish the co-launched Oscar 2 / 1964-083D. Request records already open to researchers before pursuing classification review.

NARA's [Bureau of Naval Weapons disposition schedule](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/departments/department-of-defense/department-of-the-navy/rg-0402/n1-402-89-001_sf115.pdf) identifies **Record Group 402**. Its [declassification/indexing list](https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/ndc/military-entries-that-are-potential-iod-candidates-as-of-5-16-2024.pdf), p. 116, names **entry UD-UP 134 / record-entry ID 341709**, “Confidential and Unclassified Technical Reports [Technical Library Division, Technical Reference Files, 1967],” with document dates 1940–1965. This is a concrete *finding-aid lead*, not proof that it contains the 5E-5 telemetry design. Ask NARA or NHHC whether an item-level index of this entry lists 5E-5, 5E-2, APL, the 136.65-MHz telemetry system, or report numbers from the Space Department. The same list's **UD-UP 77 / ID 341531** concerns equatorial satellite facilities and tactical range communications (1961–1964), likely less direct for the onboard bit map.

The disposition schedule also describes a broader **item 34, “Scientific and Technical Reports,” ca. 1940–1965**, 696 cubic feet of contractor and agency reports on diverse electronics and other topics, in unclassified, confidential and secret forms. Reports are ordered by stamped six- or seven-digit number or institution-based call number, and NARA recommended permanent retention. Its listed accessions include `64A-3531`, `64A-3703`, `71A-6075`, and `72A-3301` among others. Ask whether this item overlaps entry UD-UP 134; do not assume the numbering denotes the same collection. Even if held there, a 5E-5 report would need to be found by an item-level index or report number before a targeted review request.

If a specific located item is withheld as classified, [NARA's Mandatory Declassification Review guidance](https://www.archives.gov/isoo/training/mdr) recommends identifying the document by title, date and number and narrowing the request. A broad request for an unidentified telemetry format is therefore better handled as a **records-location inquiry first**, followed by MDR only for an identified classified item. The already-held APL reports TG-890 and SDO-1600 were published for unlimited distribution / as unclassified, so their missing channel map is not evidence that the map itself was classified.
